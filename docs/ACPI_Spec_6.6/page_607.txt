Once control is returned to the OS, it can handle as it sees fit - likely just re-evaluating the idle state on both cores.
When requests are received out of order, some overhead is introduced by rejecting the command and forcing the OS to
re-evaluate, but this is expected to be rare. Requests sent by the OS should be seen by the platform in the same order
the vast majority of the time, and in this case, the idle command will proceed as normal.
It is possible that the OS may choose to keep a particular hierarchy node running even if all CPUs underneath it are
asleep. This gives rise to another potential corner case - see below.
